# Laws of Human Nature — Vocabulary

## The Law of Irrationality

- irrationality — lack of clear, logical thinking  
  - Hindi: अतार्किकता  
  - Example 1: Irrationality often makes people defend bad decisions.  
  - Example 2: His anger showed more irrationality than reason.

- veer — to change direction suddenly  
  - Hindi: मुड़ जाना / दिशा बदलना  
  - Example 1: People often veer toward ideas that comfort their ego.  
  - Example 2: The discussion veered away from the real issue.

- soothe — to calm or comfort  
  - Hindi: शांत करना / दिलासा देना  
  - Example 1: He chose words that soothed her anxiety.  
  - Example 2: Many beliefs survive because they soothe fear.

- counteract — to reduce or oppose the effect of something  
  - Hindi: प्रतिकार करना / प्रभाव कम करना  
  - Example 1: Rational thinking can counteract emotional impulses.  
  - Example 2: Exercise helped counteract stress.

- cultivate — to develop deliberately  
  - Hindi: विकसित करना  
  - Example 1: Rationality is a power we must cultivate.  
  - Example 2: She cultivated the habit of careful observation.

- ultimatum — a final demand with consequences  
  - Hindi: अंतिम चेतावनी / अल्टीमेटम  
  - Example 1: Sparta delivered an ultimatum to Athens.  
  - Example 2: The manager gave him an ultimatum.

- oligarchy — rule by a small powerful group  
  - Hindi: अल्पतंत्र / कुलीनतंत्र  
  - Example 1: Sparta led a confederation of oligarchies.  
  - Example 2: The country was controlled by an oligarchy.

- preeminent — more important than others  
  - Hindi: श्रेष्ठ / सर्वप्रमुख  
  - Example 1: Athens was a preeminent commercial power.  
  - Example 2: She became the preeminent expert on the subject.

- impending — about to happen  
  - Hindi: आसन्न / निकट  
  - Example 1: A sense of impending doom spread through the city.  
  - Example 2: They felt an impending crisis.

- agitation — nervous excitement or disturbance  
  - Hindi: व्याकुलता / उत्तेजना  
  - Example 1: The crowd was in a state of agitation.  
  - Example 2: His voice revealed inner agitation.

- embolden — to make someone more confident or daring  
  - Hindi: साहसी बनाना / हौसला बढ़ाना  
  - Example 1: A weak response would embolden the enemy.  
  - Example 2: Early success emboldened him to take more risk.

- peculiar — strange or unusual  
  - Hindi: अजीब / विचित्र  
  - Example 1: Pericles seemed like a peculiar leader.  
  - Example 2: There was something peculiar in her tone.

- unobtrusive — not attracting attention  
  - Hindi: बिना दिखावे का / अनाकर्षक  
  - Example 1: His style was quiet and unobtrusive.  
  - Example 2: She worked in an unobtrusive way.

- appraisal — careful evaluation  
  - Hindi: मूल्यांकन  
  - Example 1: The book offers a realistic appraisal of human nature.  
  - Example 2: His appraisal of the situation was accurate.

- gamut — the complete range  
  - Hindi: पूरा दायरा / समूचा विस्तार  
  - Example 1: The chapter covers the full gamut of human emotion.  
  - Example 2: The film explored a wide gamut of feelings.

- permeable — easily influenced or affected  
  - Hindi: भेद्य / आसानी से प्रभावित  
  - Example 1: Humans are highly permeable to the emotions of others.  
  - Example 2: A tense room can make everyone more permeable to fear.

- susceptibility — tendency to be affected by something  
  - Hindi: प्रभावित होने की प्रवृत्ति / संवेदनशीलता  
  - Example 1: Our susceptibility to emotion shapes our behavior.  
  - Example 2: Children show high susceptibility to influence.

- propensity — natural tendency  
  - Hindi: प्रवृत्ति / झुकाव  
  - Example 1: Humans have a propensity to compare themselves with others.  
  - Example 2: He had a propensity for overthinking.

- grandiosity — exaggerated sense of greatness  
  - Hindi: अतिशयोक्त महानता / भव्यताग्रस्तता  
  - Example 1: Sudden success can lead to grandiosity.  
  - Example 2: Power fed his grandiosity.

- conformity — acting like the group  
  - Hindi: अनुरूपता / भीड़ के अनुसार चलना  
  - Example 1: Conformity can weaken independent thought.  
  - Example 2: Peer pressure often produces conformity.

- decipher — to interpret or understand  
  - Hindi: अर्थ निकालना / समझना  
  - Example 1: The book is a codebook for deciphering behavior.  
  - Example 2: He tried to decipher her expression.

- inadvertently — without intending to  
  - Hindi: अनजाने में  
  - Example 1: We often inadvertently trigger another person’s insecurity.  
  - Example 2: I inadvertently offended him.

- compendium — a collection of examples or facts  
  - Hindi: संकलन / संग्रह  
  - Example 1: The crash became a compendium of human irrationality.  
  - Example 2: The guide is a useful compendium of ideas.

- leverage — use of borrowed resources for gain  
  - Hindi: उधारी आधारित निवेश / लीवरेज  
  - Example 1: Cheap credit led to excess leverage.  
  - Example 2: Too much leverage increased the risk.

- derivatives — financial contracts based on other assets  
  - Hindi: व्युत्पन्न वित्तीय साधन  
  - Example 1: Complex derivatives made risk difficult to judge.  
  - Example 2: Traders misused derivatives in the market.

- cabal — secret group working together  
  - Hindi: गुट / गुप्त मंडली  
  - Example 1: Some blamed a corrupt cabal of insiders.  
  - Example 2: Rumors spread about a cabal in the office.

- speculation — risky financial betting  
  - Hindi: सट्टा / अटकल आधारित निवेश  
  - Example 1: Speculation fueled the financial bubble.  
  - Example 2: Excess speculation can end badly.

- primal — basic and ancient  
  - Hindi: आदिम / मूलभूत  
  - Example 1: A trigger can awaken primal fear.  
  - Example 2: The sound created a primal reaction.

- demagogue — leader who manipulates the emotions of the masses  
  - Hindi: जन-उत्तेजक नेता / भड़काऊ नेता  
  - Example 1: A demagogue keeps people emotionally inflamed.  
  - Example 2: The speech sounded more like a demagogue’s performance.

- introspection — examining one’s own thoughts and motives  
  - Hindi: आत्मचिंतन  
  - Example 1: Rationality requires introspection.  
  - Example 2: Journaling encouraged introspection.

- skepticism — questioning attitude  
  - Hindi: संशयवाद / संदेहपूर्ण दृष्टि  
  - Example 1: Healthy skepticism prevents blind belief.  
  - Example 2: Rationality needs skepticism.

- arousal — state of heightened alertness or emotion  
  - Hindi: उत्तेजना / जागरूकता  
  - Example 1: Fear begins as bodily arousal.  
  - Example 2: The speech created emotional arousal.

- orthodoxy — accepted belief system  
  - Hindi: रूढ़ मान्यता / प्रचलित मत

## The Law of Narcissism

- empathy — ability to understand and feel another person’s emotions  
  - Hindi: सहानुभूति / संवेदनशील समझ  
  - Example 1: Empathy helps us connect deeply with others.  
  - Example 2: Good leaders use empathy to understand people.

- narcissism — excessive self-focus or self-absorption  
  - Hindi: आत्ममुग्धता / स्व-आसक्ति  
  - Example 1: The chapter explains narcissism as a spectrum.  
  - Example 2: Too much narcissism weakens real connection.

- self-absorption — being overly focused on oneself  
  - Hindi: आत्म-लीनता  
  - Example 1: Self-absorption can block empathy.  
  - Example 2: His self-absorption made him ignore others’ needs.

- validation — approval that makes someone feel valued  
  - Hindi: मान्यता / स्वीकृति  
  - Example 1: Many people seek validation from others.  
  - Example 2: Social media often becomes a source of validation.

- fleeting — lasting for a very short time  
  - Hindi: क्षणिक / अल्पकालिक  
  - Example 1: Moments of praise are often fleeting.  
  - Example 2: Success can feel exciting but fleeting.

- discrepancy — difference between two things that should match  
  - Hindi: असंगति / अंतर  
  - Example 1: Others may notice the discrepancy in our self-image.  
  - Example 2: There was a discrepancy between his words and actions.

- coherent — clear, connected, and consistent  
  - Hindi: सुसंगत / स्पष्ट  
  - Example 1: A coherent self helps create stability.  
  - Example 2: She gave a coherent explanation of her feelings.

- thermostat — regulator that keeps balance; used here metaphorically  
  - Hindi: नियंत्रक / संतुलन साधने वाला  
  - Example 1: Self-esteem acts like an inner thermostat.  
  - Example 2: His confidence worked like a thermostat in hard times.

- extrovert — a person who is outgoing and socially expressive  
  - Hindi: बहिर्मुखी  
  - Example 1: Some extroverts attract attention easily.  
  - Example 2: As an extrovert, she enjoyed speaking in groups.

- introvert — a person more inward and reserved  
  - Hindi: अंतर्मुखी  
  - Example 1: Introverts may retreat into fantasy more easily.  
  - Example 2: He is an introvert who prefers quiet settings.

- exhibitionistic — behaving in a way meant to attract attention  
  - Hindi: प्रदर्शनप्रिय  
  - Example 1: Deep narcissists can become exhibitionistic.  
  - Example 2: Her exhibitionistic style always drew attention.

- grandiose — unrealistically impressive or exaggerated  
  - Hindi: भव्यताग्रस्त / अतिशयोक्तिपूर्ण  
  - Example 1: He had a grandiose sense of his own importance.  
  - Example 2: Grandiose plans often ignore reality.

- paranoia — extreme suspicion or fear of others  
  - Hindi: परानोइया / पागलपन जैसा संदेह  
  - Example 1: Insecurity can turn into paranoia.  
  - Example 2: His paranoia made him distrust everyone.

- oversensitive — too easily hurt or offended  
  - Hindi: अत्यधिक संवेदनशील  
  - Example 1: Deep narcissists are often oversensitive to criticism.  
  - Example 2: She became oversensitive after repeated rejection.

- vengeance — revenge  
  - Hindi: प्रतिशोध  
  - Example 1: He reacted with vengeance after the insult.  
  - Example 2: Their need for vengeance ruined the relationship.

- manipulative — controlling others in a clever or unfair way  
  - Hindi: चालाकी से नियंत्रित करने वाला  
  - Example 1: Manipulative people use emotions to control others.  
  - Example 2: His manipulative behavior damaged trust.

- enmeshed — overly involved or trapped in someone else’s emotional world  
  - Hindi: उलझा हुआ / अत्यधिक जुड़ा हुआ  
  - Example 1: A child can become enmeshed with a parent.  
  - Example 2: She felt enmeshed in his personal drama.

- tyrannical — cruelly controlling and oppressive  
  - Hindi: अत्याचारी / दमनकारी  
  - Example 1: Tyrannical leaders often show deep narcissism.  
  - Example 2: The office became unbearable under his tyrannical style.

- charisma — strong personal appeal that attracts others  
  - Hindi: करिश्मा / व्यक्तित्व आकर्षण  
  - Example 1: Charisma helped him gain followers.  
  - Example 2: Her charisma made people trust her quickly.

- façade — false outward appearance  
  - Hindi: मुखौटा / बाहरी दिखावा  
  - Example 1: We must see through the façade of confidence.  
  - Example 2: His kindness was only a façade.

- resilient — able to recover quickly  
  - Hindi: लचीला / जल्दी संभलने वाला  
  - Example 1: Healthy narcissists are more resilient.  
  - Example 2: Resilient people recover from insults faster.

- attunement — close awareness of another person’s emotions  
  - Hindi: भाव-सामंजस्य / भावनात्मक तालमेल  
  - Example 1: Empathy requires emotional attunement.  
  - Example 2: Her attunement to others made her a great listener.

- visceral — deeply felt in the body, instinctive  
  - Hindi: भीतर से महसूस होने वाला / सहज  
  - Example 1: Visceral empathy begins with sensing emotions physically.  
  - Example 2: He had a visceral reaction to her sadness.

- analytic — based on careful thinking and examination  
  - Hindi: विश्लेषणात्मक  
  - Example 1: Analytic empathy uses information and observation.  
  - Example 2: She took an analytic approach to understanding him.

- attribution bias — judging others’ actions as character flaws while excusing our own  
  - Hindi: आरोपण पक्षपात  
  - Example 1: Attribution bias makes us unfair in judgment.  
  - Example 2: He blamed her nature, showing attribution bias.

- rapport — close and harmonious understanding  
  - Hindi: तालमेल / अपनापन  
  - Example 1: Good listening creates rapport.  
  - Example 2: The teacher quickly built rapport with students.

- mimic — to copy another person’s expression or behavior  
  - Hindi: नकल करना  
  - Example 1: We often unconsciously mimic people we like.  
  - Example 2: Babies mimic facial expressions very early.

- indulgence — tolerant or forgiving treatment  
  - Hindi: छूट / उदार सहनशीलता  
  - Example 1: Give others the same indulgence you give yourself.  
  - Example 2: Parents sometimes show too much indulgence.

- latent — hidden but present  
  - Hindi: सुप्त / छिपा हुआ  
  - Example 1: Latent empathy can be strengthened through practice.  
  - Example 2: The experience awakened a latent ability.

- innumerable — too many to count  
  - Hindi: असंख्य  
  - Example 1: Empathy has innumerable benefits.  
  - Example 2: There were innumerable small signals to read.

- therapeutic — emotionally healing  
  - Hindi: उपचारात्मक / राहत देने वाला  
  - Example 1: Deep understanding can be therapeutic.  
  - Example 2: Listening well can be therapeutic.

- enmeshment — unhealthy emotional overinvolvement  
  - Hindi: अतिउलझाव / अत्यधिक भावनात्मक जुड़ाव  
  - Example 1: Enmeshment can stop a child from developing a self.  
  - Example 2: Their bond turned into enmeshment.

- self-object — person treated as an extension of oneself  
  - Hindi: स्वयं-वस्तु / अपने विस्तार की तरह व्यक्ति  
  - Example 1: Narcissists often treat partners as self-objects.  
  - Example 2: He saw employees as self-objects.
